# An action item has one home and carries over by staying open

**Status:** accepted — decided on [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253) via [#265](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/265). Specified, not yet built.

Carryover is why the **Team** exists ([ADR-0008](0008-a-team-is-the-permanent-visibility-boundary.md)), and the teardown is unambiguous about what goes wrong with it: "action items go nowhere" is the most consistent complaint in the category (`docs/research/retro-tool-teardown.md`, "Where they annoy users"). Retrium's items "just sit there", EasyRetro converts a card and then loses it, Parabol made action review a default phase in August 2026 and did not migrate existing teams. The one product that solved it, TeamRetro, did so by giving carryover *a place in the agenda* — `Open Actions` at the start and `Review` at the end — rather than a better form. [ADR-0010](0010-a-retro-stage-projects-and-defaults-but-never-forbids.md) already adopted that shape as the `review` stage, and [ADR-0016](0016-a-retro-is-one-room-with-its-ceremony-state-beside-it.md) made carryover a query over the team's open actions rather than a pointer between retros. This ADR fixes what an action item is, so that query has something worth returning.

The evidence for the form is thinner than the folklore, and it points toward *less*. `docs/research/retrospective-effectiveness.md` §1.6: implementation intentions survive publication-bias correction at d = .15 overall and d = .26 in workplace samples, the lowest of any sample type; the if-then form beats when-where-what (d = .43 vs .29); specifying "how" and "for how long" almost halved the effect; and goals only work with progress feedback ("goals plus feedback is more effective than goals alone"). A named owner is the best-supported accountability mechanism available because identifiability is among the strongest moderators of social loafing, and it has never been tested for action items — the spec says so rather than claiming it. §4.3 retires the cap: three plans is the *worst* cell in the only relevant table, one plan is the best, and the authors find no optimal number. And Häusser et al. keep accountability out of the generation stage, which is why an action affordance is invited late and never early.

So: **an action item is a short text with at most one named owner and an optional date, created from the topic it answers, with three states, living in exactly one retro for its whole life and carried over by the fact of still being open.** No priority, no description, no comments, no proposal workflow, no count limit, and no notification of its own.

## Considered Options

- **Text, single optional owner, optional date, source topic, three states** (chosen).
- **Text only** (rejected). Drops the one mechanism the evidence supports (a named owner) and the one that goal-setting needs (a target to give feedback against).
- **TeamRetro's full set — priority, due date, owner, propose→approve modes** (rejected). Priority is a second ranking over a list that a `vote` stage already ranked; the propose→approve mode is `actionManagement: facilitators` wearing a workflow, and [ADR-0013](0013-retro-permissions-extend-the-one-decision.md) already offers that level.
- **A required owner** (rejected). Blocks the write at the moment the room's energy is lowest, and hides the real state — unowned is information the `review` stage should show, not a validation error.
- **Several owners, or "the team" as owner** (rejected). Diffusion of responsibility (Fischer et al. g = −0.35); "the team" is unowned with a friendlier label, so it is rendered as unowned.
- **Owners restricted to permanent accounts or team members** (rejected). A teamless retro has no other kind of attendee, and the join policy already lets a team demand permanent accounts if it wants owners it can find later.
- **Copying open actions into the new retro at `review`** (rejected). Two rows for one commitment, and the status of the copy and the original drift. An action has one home; "carried over" means "still open".
- **Actions that outlive their room** (rejected). Would require a nullable `roomId` and a second ownership path for a case that only arises when someone deliberately deletes a permanent retro. The cascade stands and the confirmation names what it takes.
- **A soft cap or "too many actions" warning** (rejected on §4.3).
- **Overdue and assigned emails** (deferred, not decided here). Reminders are a notification mechanism, and the map has one notification ticket; this ADR hands it the question rather than inventing a second channel.

## Consequences

- **Shape.** `retroActions` carries `roomId`, an optional denormalised `teamId`, `text` (required), `ownerId` (optional, a user id), `dueAt` (optional date — AgileKit has no sprint object, so there is no "target sprint"), `source` (optional topic ref, `{ kind: "card" | "cluster", id }`, nulled when the cluster is dissolved or the card deleted), `status: "open" | "done" | "dropped"`, an optional `note` written only when the status leaves `open`, `createdBy`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`. Indexes stay as ADR-0016 fixed them: `by_room` and `by_team_status`. No priority, no description, no comment thread, no editor trail (the same reasoning as ADR-0012's for cards: a trail is a surface with no evidenced benefit).
- **Ownership is zero or one person, always named.** Any room member may be owner, anonymous accounts included. An anonymous account's user row is deleted on sign-out (`deleteUserByAuthUserId`, `convex/model/users.ts`), so the owner then renders as "Former member" by reference, exactly as a card author does; anonymous-to-permanent linking re-points `ownerId` alongside `authorId` and `voterId`. Anyone the `actionManagement` category allows may assign someone else, with no accept step — Locke & Latham find assigned goals equal participative ones when the rationale is given, and the source topic is the rationale. The owner may always edit, complete, drop or reopen their own item (ADR-0013's standing right).
- **Unowned is a visible state, never an error.** Rendered as "Nobody owns this yet", surfaced in the `close` panel and in `review`, never blocking.
- **Creation is never refused, and is invited late.** Per ADR-0010 a stage never forbids, so an action can be written at any stage. The affordance is foregrounded in exactly two places: on the discussion walk's current topic during `discuss` ("Add action" on the topic in focus, which fills `source`) and in the actions panel during `close`. During `collect` nothing on the board invites an action. Free-floating actions with no source are allowed.
- **No cap, no count copy.** The `close` panel states the facts — "4 actions, 1 unowned" — and nothing about too many or too few.
- **Three states, all reversible.** `open` → `done` or `dropped`, either back to `open`. `dropped` exists because "we decided not to do this" is a legitimate review outcome that is different from destroying the record; delete is a separate `actionManagement` act. The optional `note` on leaving `open` is the one place a "why" is invited, because the review moment can bear a sentence that the creation moment cannot.
- **`review` is the carryover mechanism, and it is a query.** The `review` stage lists the team's open actions created in *other* retros, oldest first, with owner and due date, and allows done, dropped, edit and reassign in place. Nothing is copied; an action has one home for life. A teamless retro's `review` is empty, and its actions expire with the room under the five-day sweep. A retro's own actions are never in its `review`.
- **An action dies with its room.** `retroActions` stays in the cascade. For a team retro that only happens on a deliberate delete, whose confirmation names the open actions it will remove.
- **Completion is manual and has three doors**: the team page, the retro's actions panel at any stage, and `review`. The `review` stage is the ask. No reminder email is specified here; whether action reminders exist is handed to [#274](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/274) as a second use of whatever notification channel it decides, and if it decides none, actions have none.
- **Visibility.** Inside a retro, one actions panel is always reachable and shows *this* retro's actions; `review` foregrounds the team's older open ones instead. Outside a retro, the team page is the only cross-retro view ([ADR-0014](0014-retro-is-the-second-ceremony-of-one-toolkit.md)); the dashboard shows none. Overdue (`dueAt` past, still `open`) is a rendering state, not a status.
- **Attribution never reaches an action** (ADR-0012): creator and owner are named in an anonymous retro. The `source` link is the one place this needs care — an action's source card in an anonymous retro is still an anonymous card, and the link renders the card's text, never an author.
- **Integration seam, specified and not built.** An action row may carry an optional `externalRef: { provider, key, url }`, and pushing an action becomes an optional capability on the provider handler in `convex/integrations/registry.ts` ([ADR-0006](0006-integration-providers-sit-behind-a-registry.md)), routed the same way webhook registration is. Building the push is out of this map's scope.
- **Tests that enforce this**: an action can be created during `collect` and the board offers no affordance for it; an unowned action saves; a second owner cannot be added; `review` returns the team's open actions from other retros and never this retro's; marking `done` from the team page updates the same row the retro shows; a teamless retro's `review` is empty; deleting a retro removes its actions; a dropped action can be reopened; the owner can complete their own item at `actionManagement: facilitators`; an anonymous owner who signs out renders as "Former member".
