# An anonymous retro card has no stored author

**Status:** accepted — decided on [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253) via [#262](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/262). Specified, not yet built.

Whether a retro card carries its author's name is the decision most responsible for whether people write the truth, and the folklore about it is wrong in a specific way. `docs/research/retrospective-effectiveness.md` §1.5 finds that anonymity reliably raises the volume and criticality of contributions and reliably does *not* improve idea or decision quality; that the status-levelling everyone credits it with is real (participation equality d = 0.80 across 48 experiments) but is delivered by the written, parallel, independent medium and not by hiding names (Rains 2005: "anonymity is not a significant moderator for any of the four influence variables"); and that it costs satisfaction and leaks — participants attempt attribution on 9–100% of anonymous items and are rarely accurate, which delivers suspicion rather than safety. The candour benefit belongs to private-until-submitted generation, which is the reveal policy's decision ([#270](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/270)), and accountability belongs to execution rather than generation (Häusser et al. 2017). The one honest case for anonymity is the one the laboratory cannot see: a power differential in the room.

So **attribution** is a per-retro property with two values, `named` and `anonymous`; **`named` is the default**; it is constant across every stage; and it **ratchets one way only**, from named to anonymous, never back. The mechanism that gives the promise teeth is that **an anonymous card stores no author at all**. The author id is absent from the row, and flipping the ratchet strips it from every existing card in one mutation — which is exactly why the ratchet is irreversible. A stored-and-hidden author would be a promise about every present and future read path; an absent one is a promise about the data.

`docs/research/retro-tool-teardown.md` is the case study for the alternative. Parabol stores authorship and projects it away at the GraphQL boundary through one per-meeting boolean, and that has leaked five times (anonymous comments de-anonymised after submission, typing indicators revealing who is writing, reactions not anonymous, @-mentions leaking into notifications, and the inverse — a named retro losing authorship on export). One flag consulted by many surfaces fails at whichever surface forgets to consult it. TeamRetro's model — a one-way ratchet toward anonymity that deliberately never anonymises action ownership — is the best in the category, and this ADR adopts both halves.

## Considered Options

- **Per-retro attribution, named by default, one-way ratchet, author genuinely not stored** (chosen).
- **Always named** (rejected). Forfeits the only defensible use of anonymity — the manager in the room — for no gain, since the default already gives a team named cards.
- **Always anonymous** (rejected). Retrium's most-praised property, and the evidence says it buys volume at the cost of satisfaction, follow-through and trust, for a levelling effect the medium already delivers.
- **Anonymous during `collect`, named after reveal** (rejected). People write believing they are unseen and are then outed. Delayed attribution is the leaky-anonymity failure with the suspicion made certain. Whatever is promised at write time stays true for the life of the card.
- **Per-card author's choice** (rejected). The single anonymous card on a named board is the most-scrutinised object in the room.
- **Stored author, projected away at the read boundary** (rejected for cards). Parabol's model, and its five leaks. It survives neither database access, nor export, nor a forgotten projection, nor a future contributor.

## Consequences

- **"Mine" in an anonymous retro is a browser-held capability, not a stored link.** The server mints a random edit key per card, stores only its hash on the card, and returns the key once; the client keeps it and presents it to edit or delete. Editing is therefore device-bound in anonymous retros, and the write-time copy says so. A per-retro pseudonym (a keyed hash of the user id) was rejected because anyone holding the database and the retro's secret re-derives every author — storage with extra steps.
- **Cards written while the retro was named and later anonymised by the ratchet have no key.** After the ratchet, only whoever card-management permission allows ([#266](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/266)) can touch them.
- **Votes are the one place "hidden" replaces "not stored".** A per-person vote budget is a server invariant that needs to know who voted, so votes always store the voter. In an anonymous retro one read-boundary projection strips it for every reader — one function, the same discipline as `sanitizeVotes` and the resolved permission decision — and the ratchet does not strip voter ids, because that would break the budget on a live retro. The promise on votes is the weaker "nobody is shown how you voted", and the UI must say the weaker thing. Any export ([#267](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/267)) goes through the same projection.
- **Anonymity is content anonymity only** (Nunamaker's distinction). Card author and vote-by-card are hidden; presence and the roster stay visible. The per-person "has written a card" signal during `collect` degrades to a total card count under anonymous, because "Sam has written one card" beside one card on the board is attribution. Typing indicators, if they ever exist, are never shown in an anonymous retro. Async nudges ([#274](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/274)) are never per-person in an anonymous retro.
- **Nobody sees attribution the team does not.** No facilitator or owner override — it is not stored, so none is possible. The person with power in the room is usually the reason a team turns this on.
- **The author always sees their own cards as theirs**, in both modes. Under `named` this is trivial; under `anonymous` it is the edit key.
- **Action items are never anonymous.** Even in an anonymous retro an action item's creator and owner are named. Their shape belongs to [#265](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/265); this ADR only fixes that anonymity stops at the card and never reaches a commitment.
- **The ratchet applies retroactively to a retro at rest.** A Team's membership changes over years, and a new member inherits read access to critique written before they arrived. Anonymising old history is the escape hatch that does not require deletion, and the concrete answer to "retention is only safe if writers know who can read later". Who may pull it belongs to [#266](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/266); it is irreversible, so it should sit at the strictest tier.
- **Attribution is by reference, not by snapshot.** A named card stores the author's user id and renders the current display name; a user row that no longer exists renders a placeholder ("Former member"). Nothing about the promise differs by account type — an anonymous-account author renders the name they typed, as everywhere else. Account deletion is [#267](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/267)'s; its starting point is that the reference dangles and renders the placeholder.
- **A card's author never changes.** Being edited, moved, clustered or tidied by someone else leaves the author untouched, and no separate editor identity is recorded in either mode. An editor trail is a leak surface with no evidenced benefit.
- **The setting is stamped at creation from a Team-level default, by copy**, exactly as the join policy is ([ADR-0008](0008-a-team-is-the-permanent-visibility-boundary.md)). A teamless retro defaults to `named`. A Team admin changing the default never rewrites a running retro's promise.
- **The write-time copy is part of the spec and may not overclaim.** Named: *"Posted as Sam. Your name stays with this card."* Anonymous: *"Anonymous. Your name is not saved with this card, not even for the facilitator. Edit or delete it from this device."* Vote UI, anonymous only: *"Nobody is shown how you voted."* Ratchet confirmation: *"Make this retro anonymous? Every author is removed permanently and this cannot be undone."* Wording may be polished; the claims may not be strengthened beyond what the storage supports.
- **For the data model ([#264](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/264)):** `attribution` on the retro; an optional `authorId` and an optional `editKeyHash` on the card, with exactly one of them present; a mandatory voter on retro votes; a default attribution on the Team.
