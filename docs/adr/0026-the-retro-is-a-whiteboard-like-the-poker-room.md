# The retro is a whiteboard, like the poker room

**Status:** accepted, 2026-09-24. Supersedes the team retro's decisions: ADR-0008, ADR-0010 to ADR-0013, ADR-0015 to ADR-0017 and ADR-0019 to ADR-0025 in full, and [ADR-0009](0009-room-access-and-room-attendance-are-separate-guards.md), [ADR-0014](0014-retro-is-the-second-ceremony-of-one-toolkit.md) and [ADR-0018](0018-the-activity-chokepoint-owns-the-clocks-precision.md) in part. Each of them opens with a status line saying what replaced it and what still holds.

The first retro AgileKit built, the team retro of [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253), shipped as an alpha and read as a second product inside the first. It had its own canvas with three zoom levels, a stamped list of six stages, named clusters and proximity hulls, a snapshotted discussion walk with raise and late cards, per-retro attribution with browser-held edit keys and a one-way ratchet, a format editor, Teams with invite links, admins, join policies and `claim`, nudge and reminder emails with a one-click unsubscribe, and team history, facts and exports. Each part had its reasons. Together they made a tool that looked, moved and was worded nothing like the poker room beside it, that a team had to learn before its first retro, and that every change had to keep correct across all of those parts at once.

So the retro is rebuilt as a whiteboard on the poker room's own canvas: the same React Flow canvas, navigation, share menu, settings side panel, presence and timer node, and the same kind of nodes. A **retro node** plays the Session node's part (the retro's name, where it is, and the one button that moves everyone on), a **pad** per column is where stickies are written from, the **stickies** sit on the board, and an **action items** node holds what the team agreed. Both ceremonies open at `/room/[roomId]` behind one join gate; `/retro/new` creates a retro and `/dashboard/retros` lists them. It keeps two ideas from the team retro: people write before they read each other ([ADR-0015](0015-a-hidden-retro-card-is-a-silhouette-projected-by-the-shared-stage.md)'s reason), and the team votes on what to talk about before it talks.

In short: a retro moves through four **steps**, `write → vote → discuss → done`, held on the room row the way poker holds its round, and moved only by a person allowed to run the retro. In `write`, everyone else's stickies reach a viewer **face-down**; moving on is the **reveal**, for everyone at once. Stickies dropped on each other form **stacks**, and in `vote` each person has a small **vote budget**, one vote per **topic**, with totals hidden until `discuss`, which takes the voted topics most-voted first with one of them in the **spotlight**. **Action items** are written on the board, and "Start the next retro" opens the next one with the open items **carried over**. A retro is **retained** once a permanent account creates it or comes to own it, by signing in, a transfer or a hand-off; every other retro is deleted after five days without activity, like a poker room.

## Considered Options

- **Simplify the team retro in place** (rejected). The complexity was structural: Teams sat under the guards, the schema and every listing, attribution under every card read and write, the stage list under every mutation. Removing any one meant touching all of them, and what was left still would not look like the poker room.
- **Keep a separate retro canvas, as [ADR-0011](0011-the-retro-board-is-one-canvas-with-semantic-zoom.md) forked it** (rejected). Two canvases to build, test and keep in step, and two surfaces for a team to learn, which is the problem this decision exists to fix.
- **The poker room's canvas, chrome and node vocabulary, with a retro node, pads and stickies** (chosen).
- **Ceremony state in a `retros` row beside the room, as [ADR-0016](0016-a-retro-is-one-room-with-its-ceremony-state-beside-it.md) had it** (rejected). The state is small (step, columns, vote budget, show authors, the spotlight, the next retro's link) and changes at a facilitator's pace, which is how poker's round state on the same row behaves. The writes that come at everyone's pace (stickies, votes, action items) are their own tables and reach the room row only through the hourly activity bump.
- **Teams for continuity** (rejected). The team retro needed an entity, a membership model and a visibility boundary to answer "what did we agree last time?". A link to the next retro and a copy of the open action items answer it.
- **An anonymous card with no stored author, as [ADR-0012](0012-an-anonymous-retro-card-has-no-stored-author.md) had it** (rejected). Every sticky stores its author and one setting, **show authors** (off by default), decides whether names are shown. Simpler to build and to explain, at the price of a weaker promise (see Consequences).
- **Emails** (rejected). A nudge or a reminder needs an addressable audience, and that was the Team. A retro is shared by its link.
- **GIF search called from the browser** (rejected). It puts the GIPHY key in the page. A Next.js route proxies the search, and a pasted link is accepted only from an allowlist of media hosts.

## Consequences

- **Steps are soft for writing and strict for voting.** In every step stickies can be written and moved, authors can edit or delete their own, and action items can be changed; a person who runs the retro can jump to any step and back, and nothing written is lost. Three things belong to one step each: another person's sticky is face-down only in `write`, and going back to `write` turns it face-down again; votes are cast only in `vote`, and anywhere else the server refuses with the `stage` code; the topics are walked only in `discuss`. While writing, a person stacks only their own stickies, and nobody but its author edits or deletes a sticky, whatever their permission level: nobody groups or moderates what they cannot read.
- **Face-down is a server-side projection.** `retro.board` returns someone else's sticky in `write` as its id, place, column and stack, never its words, GIF or author, and the step it reads is the room's, never the viewer's, so no role reads a face-down sticky early. Author names travel only after the reveal, and only while show authors is on. The room row, retro state included, stays readable by link through `rooms.get`; it holds no sticky content.
- **Show authors hides names; it does not make a sticky anonymous.** The author is always stored, so turning the setting on names every revealed sticky, including those written while it was off. Copy may say that teammates see what was written and not by whom, by default. It may never say a sticky is anonymous or that nobody can find out who wrote it.
- **A stack is one topic.** Stacks are one level deep and have no name. Dropping a stack's top on another sticky moves the whole stack; a vote on any sticky of a stack counts for the stack, so stacking and unstacking never lose a vote; deleting a stack's top promotes its oldest sticky, which keeps the stack's votes and the spotlight. This replaces clusters, hulls and tidy.
- **The vote budget is per person per retro**: `votesPerPerson` votes (3 by default, 1 to 10), at most one per topic, and voting again takes it back. While voting, a person sees their own votes and how many have been cast; totals per topic show from `discuss` on.
- **The discussion order is computed, not snapshotted.** Topics with votes, most first, ties by column order and then age; when nobody voted, every topic, column by column. Votes cannot change outside `vote`, so the order holds through `discuss`, though stacking, unstacking or deleting a sticky there can still reorder it. A person who runs the retro can put any revealed topic in the spotlight, voted for or not, which replaces [ADR-0023](0023-the-walk-covers-what-was-voted-for-and-a-person-raises-the-rest.md)'s raise; doing so during `vote` moves the retro to `discuss`. Everyone's view pans to the spotlight.
- **Carry-over is a copy.** "Start the next retro" opens the next retro once: the same columns, vote budget and show-authors setting, the number in its name moved on ("Sprint 42 retro" becomes "Sprint 43 retro"), owned by whoever pressed it. Every open action item is copied in, marked carried over, and the finished retro keeps a link to it. Ticking off the copy does not tick off the original. This reverses [ADR-0017](0017-an-action-item-has-one-home-and-carries-over-by-staying-open.md)'s one home, because without a Team there is nothing to query across retros.
- **Retention follows a permanent owner.** `rooms.retained` is set when a permanent account creates the retro, when its guest owner signs in to a permanent account, or when a transfer or a hand-off gives it to a permanent account, and it is never cleared. The daily sweep deletes every room that is not retained five days after its last activity. Deleting an account (a guest's sign-out included) hands each retro it owns to the member who joined first, or deletes the retro when nobody else joined. There is no `claim`: an owner who left a retro gets it back by rejoining.
- **One permission model.** The retro keeps [ADR-0013](0013-retro-permissions-extend-the-one-decision.md)'s four categories over the one `evaluate`, with the same defaults: `stageFlow` (steps, the discussion, the spotlight, the next retro) at `facilitators`, `cardManagement` (editing or deleting someone else's sticky, once revealed) at `facilitators`, `actionManagement` (every action-item write, including an item's owner ticking it off) at `everyone`, and `retroSettings` (name, columns, vote budget, show authors) at `facilitators`. Moving and stacking stickies is open to every member, as on any whiteboard. The relationship verbs are poker's plus `delete`, which is owner-level; `claim`, `ratchet` and the join policy are gone, and a retro has no spectators.
- **The reader guard admits room members only.** With no Teams, `requireRoomReader` and `requireRoomMember` admit the same people. They stay two guards, as [ADR-0009](0009-room-access-and-room-attendance-are-separate-guards.md) set them.
- **Every retro write still goes through the activity chokepoint at hourly precision** ([ADR-0018](0018-the-activity-chokepoint-owns-the-clocks-precision.md)). The retro's state is back on the room row, so a step change or a spotlight move re-sends `rooms.get` to every member; that happens at a facilitator's pace. Sticky, vote and action-item writes land in their own tables.
- **Writes land before the server answers.** Sticky, vote, step, spotlight and action-item writes are Convex optimistic updates, which Convex rolls back if the server refuses. The model layer refuses by rule with a `ConvexError` carrying one of `forbidden`, `budget`, `missing` or `stage`, whose message the board shows. The canvas keeps a React Flow node buffer, as the poker room does, holding a node's position locally only while it is dragged, which reverses [ADR-0022](0022-the-canvass-only-local-state-is-the-hand.md); a drop is one batched move, and a client-minted `clientId` is each sticky's node key and makes a retried create idempotent.
- **GIFs come through a proxy or an allowlist.** `src/app/api/gifs/route.ts` proxies GIPHY search and trending (rated pg-13) for any signed-in session, guests included, with `GIPHY_API_KEY` set in the Next.js environment; without the key the picker only takes a pasted link. Whatever the source, the server stores a GIF only from an https GIPHY, Tenor or Imgur media host (`normalizeGifUrl`), rewriting a GIPHY page link to its media file, so a sticky can never make teammates' browsers call an arbitrary server. Those browsers do load the images from those hosts directly.
- **No email but the magic link.** `convex/email.ts` sends the sign-in link and nothing else. `UNSUBSCRIBE_SECRET` is no longer read; `users.emailOptOut` stays in the schema, unused, until it is dropped.
- **Sharing is a Markdown summary**, copied or downloaded from the board and built in the viewer's browser from what that viewer can see, so a face-down sticky never reaches it and names appear only as action-item owners. There is no JSON history export and no share page.
- **Legacy data is purged, not migrated.** `migrations:purgeLegacyRetros` deletes every team-retro room through the room cascade, empties the team retro's tables (cancelling any reminder still scheduled) and strips `teamId` and `joinPolicy` from the remaining rooms. It runs once per deployment after this version ships; until it has, an old retro opens to "Retro Unavailable". After it, `rooms.teamId` and `rooms.joinPolicy` can be dropped from the schema.
