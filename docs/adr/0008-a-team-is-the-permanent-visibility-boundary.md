# A Team is the permanent visibility boundary

**Status:** accepted — decided on [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253) via [#257](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/257). Specified, not yet built.

Retros are permanently retained, which raises a question planning poker never had to answer: who may read a room's contents later? The research behind this map is unambiguous that retention and visibility are different features — Dingsøyr et al. observed teams toning down or removing critique once retro minutes became public, and `docs/research/retrospective-effectiveness.md` §5.3 resolves it as "retain everything; scope **visibility** to the team that produced it". The **Team** exists to be that scope. It is not a workspace-lite and it is not a billing unit: it owns retro history, and it names the set of people who may read it.

Concretely: `rooms` gains an optional `teamId` that may be set **once and never changed**, plus a `by_team` index. A team is addressed at `/team/[teamId]` (no user-chosen slug — that is a global namespace with squatting and uniqueness problems Convex does not give for free), readable only by its members, and its history is simply its rooms in creation order. Membership lives in its own `teamMemberships` table carrying a **team role** of `admin` or `member`, requires a permanent account, and is only ever created by consuming the team's rotatable invite link — never as a side effect of joining a retro.

## Considered Options

- **Optional set-once `teamId` on `rooms`** (chosen). A room belongs to at most one team, so a join table would encode a cardinality that must never occur and add a read to every permission path. Putting it on `rooms` rather than a retro-only table is what makes adopting existing poker rooms into teams a one-field change later, with no relationship migration. Set-once because re-homing a room moves its history across a visibility boundary, and un-teaming would silently re-arm the five-day sweep on data users were told was permanent.
- **A separate retro-series entity** (rejected). Parabol has `meetingSeriesId` and TeamRetro has recurring meetings, but the team already *is* the series here. A series earns its place only when per-series config exists (cadence, pinned format), which this map has not reached.
- **Extending `roomMemberships` with a nullable `roomId`** (rejected). Every index on that table is keyed by `roomId`, it sits in `ROOM_OWNED_TABLES` so it is inside the room-delete cascade's blast radius, and it carries `isSpectator`, which is meaningless team-wide. It is also the table `requireRoomMember` funnels through; that guard must stay a single unbranched indexed lookup.
- **Allowing anonymous team membership** (rejected). An anonymous identity is a browser session. "Whoever held this cookie in March" is not a knowable reader, which is precisely what the visibility boundary has to guarantee. Anonymous users are *not* shut out of team retros — they join rooms by link and participate fully; they simply are not in the Team, so they do not get the history.

## Consequences

- **A Team can never be left without an admin.** The last admin must promote someone or delete the team before leaving. This deliberately diverges from [ADR-0001](0001-lockdown-is-a-denial-reason-not-a-gate.md), which tolerates an ownerless room as a **lockdown** denial reason. Rooms may be abandoned because they are ephemeral — five days and they are gone. A Team is permanent by construction, so an admin-less Team is a permanent object nobody can administer, invite into, or delete, sitting on retro history forever. That is a leak, not a denial reason. Auto-promoting the longest-standing member was rejected as handing someone powers they never asked for over other people's candid material.
- **The five-day sweep must learn about retained rooms.** `by_activity` is `["lastActivityAt"]` with no discriminator (`convex/model/cleanup.ts`), so it cannot currently tell a retained retro from a stale poker room. The mechanism is [#267](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/267)'s to settle; note that a compound index over an unbackfilled field matches nothing in Convex, so this needs a staged widen → backfill → narrow.
- **The team name is exposed on the unguarded room read, deliberately.** `rooms.get` performs no authorization, so anything on the room doc is readable by anyone holding the link. That is correct here: someone typing a candid card needs to see "this board is kept by Acme Squad" *before* they type it. Write-time disclosure is what makes permanent retention safe. What stays guarded is the team's other retros, its member list, and the team page.
- **Removal is an access operation and never a content operation.** Deleting a departed member's cards or scrubbing their attribution would rewrite a record a group made decisions from, and would destroy the named ownership that action-item follow-through depends on. Team removal deletes the `teamMemberships` row and nothing else — it does not eject anyone from a live room, since that is the existing per-room `remove` relationship action and a team admin holds no room powers. Erasure as a data-subject right is a different trigger with a different scope and belongs to [#267](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/267).
- **The per-room join policy takes a team-level default by copy, not by reference.** The room's stored value is authoritative, exactly as `permissions` works today (`getEffectivePermissions` is `room.permissions ?? DEFAULT_PERMISSIONS`). Reading a team default through at check time would let an admin silently rewrite the admission rules of an open retro with anonymous people already inside it.
- **`linkAnonymousToPermanent` needs no change.** Because anonymous identities cannot hold team memberships and joining a retro never joins a team, an upgraded user keeps their room memberships, keeps access to the retros they attended, and simply becomes invitable.
