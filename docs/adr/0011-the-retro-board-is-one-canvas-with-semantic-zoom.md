# The retro board is one canvas with semantic zoom

**Status:** accepted — decided on [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253) via [#261](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/261). Specified, not yet built.

Three board directions were prototyped in parallel and compared side by side ([#258](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/258) structured columns, [#259](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/259) guided single-focus, [#260](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/260) spatial affinity canvas). The canvas won. A retro's most valuable output is *"these five cards are the same complaint"*, and free 2D placement is the only one of the three that states that as a fact you read in half a second rather than as a list you assemble in your head. It is also the direction that shares a visual language with the existing planning-poker room, so a team moving between the two ceremonies perceives no mode change.

The canvas has one structural defect, and this ADR is mostly about how it is paid for: **at readable zoom you see a handful of cards, and at fit-to-board zoom you can read none of them.** The prototype measured 27% zoom to fit 40 cards. A retro is forty minutes of shared attention, so a surface where nobody can see the shape of what the group collectively wrote is working against the ceremony.

A second, non-spatial "twin" view was designed as the answer and then **rejected**. The retro is one board. Legibility is instead carried by **zoom level**: cards render as full *detail* above ~70%, as a clamped *headline* between ~35% and ~70%, and as tinted *shape* blocks below that — where named **cluster** labels hold constant screen size and become the content. Zooming out changes resolution rather than losing information, so the board reads as a whole at the exact moment you need it to. This is what Miro and FigJam do and what the prototype conspicuously did not; its own notes attribute its poor zoomed-out state to precisely that omission.

## Considered Options

- **Spatial affinity canvas, one view, with semantic zoom** (chosen). Keeps a single surface and a single mental model, makes the overview spatially truthful rather than a parallel rendering, and puts **discussion walk** coverage on the board instead of in a side panel.
- **Structured columns** (rejected). Legible immediately and the best answer to "where do I write this down", but the prototype demonstrated that a stage is *invisible* on a board where everything is always on screen — with emphasis disabled the rendered board was byte-identical across `group`, `vote` and `discuss`. Worse, a group lives inside exactly one column, so grouping a complaint with its proposed fix physically drags the idea card out of the column it was written in. The columns are a write-time affordance that becomes wrong the moment grouping does its job.
- **Guided single-focus flow** (rejected). Its prototype established something worth keeping even though the direction lost: focus is a *layout* property, not a *permission* property, so [ADR-0010](0010-a-retro-stage-projects-and-defaults-but-never-forbids.md)'s soft machine does not rule out single-focus. It also surfaced that a card written late is accepted with no friction and then structurally buried, which is a gap independent of which board won.
- **Canvas plus a non-spatial twin** (rejected). An outline view over the same data, where the walk, coverage, voting and grouping all also worked, would have answered legibility, mobile and keyboard grouping in one build. Rejected because two renderings of one retro is two products: every board feature has to be designed, built and kept correct twice, and the second view steadily becomes the real one for anybody who prefers lists. One board, made to work at every scale, is the smaller and more honest commitment.

## Consequences

- **Mobile is the same canvas, and this is a bet on unclaimed ground.** No canvas tool in the category has made it work: FigJam retired its iPad app, Miro's voting excludes phones, Retrium "is not tailored for the small screen", and Spreo ships no mobile or tablet support at all (`docs/research/retro-tool-teardown.md:805-807`). We are betting that semantic zoom plus touch-first interaction clears a bar nobody else has cleared. It is load-bearing that the phone's dominant use case is **async collection**, where writing a card needs no spatial reasoning at all. Chrome collapses to a full-bleed canvas with one bottom sheet, a single stage pill, no minimap, and one card-creation button.
- **On touch, the primary grouping gesture is tap-select-then-group, not drag.** The teardown identifies non-drag grouping as unclaimed across the entire category, and drag-on-touch is what every incumbent fails at. This is only expressible because a cluster is an identity rather than a location.
- **A cluster never moves its members.** Select-then-group tints and labels scattered cards instead of yanking them to a centroid, since position on a canvas is content somebody authored. **Tidy** is the explicit opt-in that gathers them.
- **Proximity hulls appear only during the `group` stage.** They are transient, have no identity, and dissolve when a member moves. Leaving them drawn during `discuss` is noise.
- **React Flow carries it, but as a new integration rather than a reuse.** It is already a dependency at `12.11.2` and supplies pan/zoom, marquee, `setCenter`/`fitBounds` for the walk, `onlyRenderVisibleElements`, and `parentId`/`extent: 'parent'` for clusters. The existing room canvas exercises almost none of that: it is a static diagram renderer over ~26 bounded nodes with inert system-owned edges, no selection handling, no grouping (`parentId` and `extent: 'parent'` have zero uses anywhere in `src/` or `convex/`), and no viewport API beyond `fitView`/`zoomIn`/`zoomOut`.
- **Three specific things must not be inherited.** `translateExtent` is a hard 4000×4000 cage (`src/components/room/room-canvas.tsx:265-268`) against a 40-card seed already spanning ~3500×2600. `panOnDrag={[1, 2]}` binds panning to middle and right mouse button only, which is wrong for the trackpad users this direction is built for. And `useNodeDragBuffer.ts:49-52` replaces the local buffer wholesale on every subscription tick with no exemption for the node under the cursor — masked today by a small set of mostly-locked nodes, and exactly the seam that forty free cards and eight draggers would unmask. Write a fresh drag and persistence path.
- **Every card drag is a write, and nudging is the core gesture.** On a column board a drag ending in the same slot is a no-op; on a canvas it always lands on new float coordinates, and "move it 20px closer so it joins the hull" is how this direction is used. Settle-only writes are mandatory and mid-drag streaming is never shipped. The mitigations belong elsewhere: subscription splitting and payload projection to [#264](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/264), the activity-chokepoint exemption to [#269](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/269), and optimistic updates to their own ticket — the repo currently has no `withOptimisticUpdate` anywhere.
- **Poker and retro share design language and components, not canvas code.** Same tokens, same shadcn/Base UI components, same `Background` dots and drag feel. The canvas seam itself is forked.
- **Coverage is a property of the board, not a readout.** At shape level, coverage is which cluster labels are still un-ticked. This is only affordable because zooming out stays legible, and it makes the strongest finding in the effectiveness research (information coverage against decision quality at r = .56, versus discussion focus at r = .25) something you can see rather than something you are told.
