# Retro permissions extend the one decision, and a team admin holds one room power

**Status:** accepted — decided on [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253) via [#266](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/266). Specified, not yet built.

The repo already has one authorization model: a pure **permission decision** (`evaluate` in `convex/permissions.ts`) of role × permission level × target role × lockdown, fed by one **permission guard** (`requireCan`), with three **roles** and four owner-configurable **permission categories**. Reading it again for retro shows that only two things in it are poker-specific — the *names* of the four categories and the shape of the `permissions` object on `rooms`. The decision function itself is generic over a category name and a level, and never mentions cards, issues or reveals. So a retro does not need a second permission system; it needs its own category set plugged into the same decision, plus answers to three questions poker never faced: what a permanent room does when its owner is gone, how admission is controlled, and what a **Team** admin may do inside a room.

The answers, in one paragraph. **Roles are unchanged** — owner, facilitator, participant, with the same relationship verbs; retro facilitation being more active is a matter of *defaults*, not of a fourth role. **Categories are per room type**: poker keeps `revealCards / gameFlow / issueManagement / roomSettings`, retro gets `stageFlow / cardManagement / actionManagement / retroSettings`, and `PermissionCategory` becomes a union keyed by `roomType` while `evaluate` stays untouched. **Card rights are three-tiered**: your own card is always yours, grouping is always everyone's, and touching another person's card is `cardManagement`. **Admission** is a per-room `joinPolicy` with its own small pure decision beside `evaluate`, not inside it. And **a team admin holds exactly one room power** — `claim`, taking ownership of a team room that would otherwise be unadministrable — which is the room-level form of ADR-0008's rule that a permanent object may never be left without someone who can administer it.

## Considered Options

- **Reuse the four poker category names and reinterpret them for retro** (rejected). "Reveal cards" means nothing in a retro — reveal is a stage default owned by the reveal policy ([#270](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/270)), not a category — and every settings panel and denial message would be lying about what it governs.
- **Generalise both room types onto abstract category names** (rejected). It forces poker's settings UI to be re-labelled for no user benefit and hides the fact that the two ceremonies genuinely gate different acts.
- **A category set per room type over one `evaluate`** (chosen). The decision was already generic; only the vocabulary and the stored shape needed to fork.
- **A fourth role, or a distinct "retro facilitator"** (rejected). Nothing in the retro decisions needs a rank between facilitator and owner, and a role that exists in one room type breaks the shared relationship verbs.
- **Plain lockdown for retro rooms, as for poker** (rejected). ADR-0001 tolerates an ownerless room because rooms expire in five days. A team retro is permanent, so an absent owner leaves a dead `changePerms`, a dead **ratchet** and — because team removal never touches `roomMemberships` — possibly an ex-member as owner forever. That is the leak ADR-0008 refused to allow for Teams, one level down.
- **Team admins as ambient owner-level in every team room** (rejected). Contradicts ADR-0008's "a team admin holds no room powers", and lets an admin override a live retro's facilitator by rank alone.
- **Join policy as a branch of `evaluate`** (rejected). A joiner has no membership and no role; `evaluate` is a decision about members. Bolting a pre-membership check onto it would give the function a second subject.
- **Join policy checked inline in `joinRoom`** (rejected). The join page needs the same verdict for its disabled state and message; two copies drift, which is exactly the failure `resolve` was built to end.

## Consequences

- **`PermissionCategory` is a union by room type**, and `rooms.permissions` becomes a discriminated union on `roomType` — poker rooms keep their four keys, retro rooms carry `stageFlow`, `cardManagement`, `actionManagement`, `retroSettings`. `getEffectivePermissions(room)` returns the set for the room's type; `evaluate`, `resolve`, `denialMessage` and `requireCan` do not change shape. The frontend's `canDoAction` selects its category by room type the same way.
- **What each retro category governs.** `stageFlow`: **advance** (both directions), editing the **stage list** (skip, reorder, timebox), moving the **discussion walk** cursor and marking coverage, and whatever "raise this now" affordance [#277](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/277) settles on. `cardManagement`: editing the text of, deleting, or moving *another person's* card; **tidy** (it rewrites authored positions); renaming or dissolving a **cluster**. `actionManagement`: creating, editing, deleting or reassigning action items other than your own. `retroSettings`: renaming the retro, editing the **join policy**, and format-level toggles.
- **Retro defaults differ from poker's.** Poker defaults every category to `everyone` for back-compat; retro has none, so the defaults are chosen: `stageFlow: facilitators`, `cardManagement: facilitators`, `actionManagement: everyone`, `retroSettings: facilitators`. Advance moves everyone's shared pointer, and a participant advancing mid-`collect` is the one disruption ADR-0010's free navigation does not absorb; deleting or rewriting someone else's card default-open is wrong in a candour ceremony; gating action creation makes the facilitator a bottleneck on the one thing the evidence says buys follow-through (named ownership, `docs/research/retrospective-effectiveness.md` §1.6). A solo owner is unaffected: `facilitators` includes the owner.
- **Three tiers of card rights.** *Self, never restricted*: write, edit, delete and move your own card — proven by `authorId` in a named retro and by the **edit key** in an anonymous one (ADR-0012). *Everyone, never restricted*: forming a cluster from a selection and adding or removing cluster membership — grouping is the participatory act, and gating it recreates TeamRetro's "only the facilitator can group" lockstep, the thing ADR-0011's tap-select-then-group exists to avoid. *`cardManagement`*: everything done to another person's card or to a cluster's identity. A facilitator can delete a card mid-retro through this category; link-join makes moderation a real need, and a stage never forbids. Cards anonymised by the ratchet have no key, so after it only `cardManagement` can touch them.
- **Never in the config.** Write a card, vote within budget, set **readiness**, navigate your own view away from the shared pointer, complete or edit an action item assigned to you, leave. Writing and voting are the ceremony; the assignee-can-always-complete rule is the accountability half of ADR-0012. Whether an action item can be *proposed* and then approved is the action-item ticket's ([#265](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/265)) lifecycle question; this ADR only fixes the category and the assignee's standing right.
- **No spectator in retro v1.** `isSpectator` stays on the membership row unused, the retro join form hides the toggle, and the observer is the non-attending team reader of ADR-0009. If the data model ([#264](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/264)) ever needs a no-budget attendee, it adds one deliberately.
- **`joinPolicy` is per room with three values**: `anyone`, `permanentAccounts`, `teamMembers` — the last offered only when the room has a `teamId`. Teamless rooms default to `anyone`; team rooms copy the Team's default (ADR-0008's copy-not-reference rule). A **team member satisfies every value**: access is the stronger claim, and someone who may read the archive may sit in the room.
- **The join decision is its own pure function.** `evaluateJoin(policy, accountType, isTeamMember)` sits beside `evaluate`, returns the same `{ allowed } | { allowed: false, reason }` shape with its own reasons (`permanent-account-required`, `team-members-only`), and has one adapter in `joinRoom` (`convex/model/users.ts`) before the membership insert. The join page calls the same function for its disabled state and copy, exactly as `resolve` serves both the guard's throw and the frontend tooltip. It is not a branch of `evaluate`.
- **`claim` is a new relationship verb and the only room power a team role confers.** A team admin who is a member of a team room may take ownership of it when the current owner is **absent** (ADR-0001's lockdown condition) **or is no longer a member of the team**. A present, in-team owner must `transfer`; the denial reason is a new `owner-present` with copy "The owner is still here — ask them to transfer ownership." `DecisionContext` gains `actorTeamRole?: "admin" | "member"`, populated by the guard only for rooms with a `teamId`, and `ownerInTeam: boolean` alongside `ownerAbsent`. Because every mutation keeps `requireRoomMember` (ADR-0009), the admin joins first — always possible, since a team member passes any join policy. On success the previous owner's membership, if it exists, becomes `participant`, keeping ADR-0001's invariant that exactly one owner-role membership exists iff the owner is present. Teamless retros keep plain lockdown; they expire.
- **The ratchet is owner-only and non-configurable**: a relationship verb `ratchet`, listed in `requiresOwnerLevel`, so an absent owner yields the `owner-absent` reason and, in a team room, the route to anonymising history is `claim` then `ratchet`. Join policy, by contrast, is a `retroSettings` category action, which is what makes the locked "owner/facilitator may require permanent accounts" true at the `facilitators` level.
- **A Team carries one retro-defaults bundle**, `{ attribution, joinPolicy, permissions }`, edited by a team admin on the team page and copied by value onto every new team retro. One mechanism for all three, one panel, and the same rule that changing it never rewrites a running retro. Editing the bundle is a team action, guarded by team role, not by `evaluate`.
- **Any team member may create a team retro** and becomes its owner. Admins administer the boundary, not the ceremony. Teamless retros stay creatable by anyone, anonymous included.
- **Retro rooms always have an owner.** The legacy `ownerId === undefined` mode exists for pre-permissions poker rooms only; `isRoomOwnerAbsent` and the lockdown banner behave as ADR-0001 describes.
- **Room `remove` in a team retro ends attendance, not access.** A removed attendee who is still a team member keeps reading the retro through `requireRoomReader`; making them unable to read requires team removal, which is an admin's act and which ADR-0008 already keeps separate from content.
- **Tests that enforce this**: a participant cannot advance at default levels; a participant cannot delete another person's card but can delete their own and can form a cluster; an anonymous account is refused by `permanentAccounts` and `teamMembers` and a team member passes all three; a team admin cannot `claim` while the owner is present and in the team, and can once the owner has left the room or the team; a team member without admin cannot `claim` at all.
