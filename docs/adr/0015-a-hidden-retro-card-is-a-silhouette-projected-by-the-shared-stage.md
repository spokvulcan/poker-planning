# A hidden retro card is a silhouette, projected server-side by the shared stage

**Status:** accepted — decided on [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253) via [#270](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/270). Specified, not yet built.

Whether other people can read a card while it is being written is a different decision from whether it carries a name ([ADR-0012](0012-an-anonymous-retro-card-has-no-stored-author.md)), and it is the one the evidence speaks to most directly. `docs/research/retrospective-effectiveness.md` grades "independent generation before discussion beats open brainstorming" and "anchoring on what is seen first narrows the space that follows" both **A**, and its design guidance (§5.4) is blunt: do not reveal others' cards during generation, because exposure shrinks the range of things considered while leaving the *count* intact, so the damage is invisible in any metric a tool would naturally track; anchoring is immune to warnings, incentives and expertise, so sequence is the only lever; and if cards must be shown before everyone is done, "show few and group them" (Hofstetter et al. 2020). The candour that folklore credits to anonymity is delivered here, by private-until-submitted generation.

`docs/research/retro-tool-teardown.md` §1b shows the incumbents answering in opposite directions with opposite failures. Retrium blurs globally until the facilitator reveals, and fails by dead air. Spreo reveals per author, self-serve, and fails by anchoring — the first revealer sets the frame and there is never a moment the team sees the whole picture at once. EasyRetro and Parabol hide on the client only, and Miro's paid private mode was defeated by an AI catch-up feature that did not know about it. A per-stage reveal policy is named as unclaimed ground.

So a **reveal policy** is a two-valued field on every **stage entry** — `hidden` or `visible` — seeded from the format with `collect` hidden and everything else visible. **A hidden card is a silhouette**: other readers get its position and a tint, never its text or its author, and the projection happens in the server read function, so the content never leaves the server. The entry that governs is always the **shared pointer's**, never the viewer's. The only reveal is global: advancing out of `collect` is the reveal, and a `stageFlow` toggle on the current entry covers the rest. Nobody, at any role, sees more than everyone else before that.

## Considered Options

- **Global reveal, facilitator-gated, as a separate button** (Retrium; rejected as the primary path). Creates the wait-for-one-person step. Here reveal rides on **advance**, which readiness already tells the facilitator when to do; the in-place toggle exists but is not the flow.
- **Per-author self-serve reveal** (Spreo; rejected). The anchoring machine. One-card-at-a-time reveal is the read-around that §5.4 calls production blocking rebuilt in software.
- **Cards visible as they arrive** (FigJam, Miro default; rejected as the default). The board looks productive and is quietly narrower. Available as a format choice — `collect` set to `visible` — because a stage projects and never forbids, and some teams want an open board.
- **Client-side blur** (EasyRetro, Parabol; rejected). Content already on the client is content already leaked, and every new surface has to remember the flag. One server-side projection, same discipline as `sanitizeVotes` and ADR-0012's vote projection.
- **Absent rather than silhouetted** (rejected). During days of async collection an empty board reads as a dead retro. The silhouette shows the shape of what is being written — how many cards sit under each prompt at shape zoom — which is "show few and group them" applied to a board that shows nothing readable.
- **A facilitator or owner content peek** (rejected). The person with power in the room is the reason the promise exists. Same rule as ADR-0012's "nobody sees attribution the team does not".
- **A per-card "revealed at" so a card once seen stays seen** (rejected). Puts reveal state on cards and reopens the leak surface for no evidenced benefit; rewinding to `collect` is used for a second round of writing, where hiding is what you want.

## Consequences

- **The policy is data on the stage entry** — `cardsVisible: "hidden" | "visible"` — seeded by the format, so two `collect` entries carry two policies, and a retro read years later renders with the policy it was run with. It is **not** in the Team retro-defaults bundle of [ADR-0013](0013-retro-permissions-extend-the-one-decision.md); whether a Team has a default *format* belongs to the data model ([#264](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/264)).
- **The projection is a pure function of (current entry's policy, reader, card), server-side, in the retro's read function.** A hidden card that is not the reader's own is returned as position, tint and id only — no text, no author, in both attribution modes. Every reader gets it: attendees, non-attending team readers under `requireRoomReader` ([ADR-0009](0009-room-access-and-room-attendance-are-separate-guards.md)), and any export ([#267](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/267)). "Own" is `authorId` in a named retro and the **edit key** in an anonymous one.
- **The shared pointer governs, never the viewer's stage.** A person who navigates their own view ahead to `group` while the retro sits in `collect` sees silhouettes. The read function takes the stage from the retro, never from a client argument.
- **Reveal is advance.** Leaving `collect` for a `visible` entry is the reveal moment; there is no separate step in the default flow. An in-place toggle on the current entry flips the policy in either direction and is a `stageFlow` act (ADR-0013), covering "show the board without moving on" and "hide it again".
- **Time is not an input.** A card written in a `visible` stage appears to everyone at once — the late card's route back into the conversation is [#277](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/277)'s to build on this. Rewinding the pointer into a `hidden` entry hides every card again, already-seen ones included. A second `collect` entry later in the list hides again.
- **No per-author reveal exists**, which is what lets the write-time promise be one sentence.
- **Nobody peeks.** No role and no permission level reads a hidden card that is not their own. The facilitator's extra is what everyone has: silhouettes, counts per prompt, and the per-person "has written" signal in the roster of a *named* retro, which collapses to a total under `anonymous` exactly as ADR-0012 fixed.
- **Vote tallies use the same mechanism.** A second per-entry field governs whether tallies are readable, projected server-side by the same function under the same shared-pointer rule; its values and defaults belong to [#264](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/264). Neither cards nor tallies are ever hidden client-side or revealed per author.
- **Async collection follows with no extra rule.** The **collection window** is `collect`, `collect` is `hidden` by default, so cards landing over days are silhouettes until the facilitator advances. The nudge ticket ([#274](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/274)) may count cards and, in a named retro, name who has written; it may never quote one.
- **The write-time copy is part of the spec and may not overclaim.** Hidden, named: *"Only you can read this for now. Others can see you've added a card, not what it says. Everyone reads it once cards are revealed."* Hidden, anonymous: *"Only you can read this for now. Everyone reads it once cards are revealed."* Visible, either: *"Everyone in the retro can read this now."* It never says "private" and never promises a time ("until the meeting"), because a facilitator may flip the toggle at any moment; "until cards are revealed" is the honest phrase. It stacks with ADR-0012's attribution line.
- **Tests that enforce this**: a non-author reading a `collect` retro gets no card text and no author; the author gets their own in full; a reader whose own view is navigated to `group` still gets silhouettes while the shared pointer is at `collect`; advancing to `review` makes every card readable; rewinding to `collect` hides them again; a `visible` `collect` (format override) returns text; the export path returns the same projection as the board.
