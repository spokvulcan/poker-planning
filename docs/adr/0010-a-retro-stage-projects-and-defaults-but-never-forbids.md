# A retro stage projects and defaults but never forbids

**Status:** accepted — decided on [map #253](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/253) via [#263](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/263). Specified, not yet built. Amended by [ADR-0023](0023-the-walk-covers-what-was-voted-for-and-a-person-raises-the-rest.md): the walk snapshot is scoped to voted topics, and a person raises anything else into it.

A retro is a guided flow, not a blank whiteboard — locked at charting. That puts AgileKit in the same archetype as Retrium, TeamRetro and Parabol, and inherits their documented failure modes along with the shape. The evidence is worth reading precisely before copying any of them, because it supports *structure*, not *enforcement*. `docs/research/retrospective-effectiveness.md` grades the Derby & Larsen ordering **Folklore, D** (`:68`) and finds no study anywhere testing a five-phase order against alternatives. What it does find (§1.7) is that complaining begets complaining while inhibiting solution-oriented statements, and that "**to inhibit complaining, the results point to the importance of structuring statements**" — the review's own verdict being that this is "the strongest, most directly applicable evidence in the review for a guided phase flow". A guided retro is a machine for emitting procedural structuring utterances. Nothing in that argument requires the machine to lock a door.

So a **stage** changes what the board foregrounds, what it prompts for, and what the defaults are while it is current. It never rejects a write. Writing, editing, grouping and voting stay open in every stage. Entering a stage with nothing in it renders an empty state with an explanation, not a locked door. **Advancing** or rewinding destroys, finalises and hides nothing — any hiding is a read-time projection owned by the reveal and anonymity decisions, never a side effect of the machine.

The mechanism that makes this workable is that the sequence is *data*: an ordered list of stage entries stamped onto the retro at creation and seeded from its format. Skipping is absence from the list, reordering is a reordered list, a per-format flow is a different seed — one mechanism instead of three, and each entry carries its own identity so per-stage state hangs off the entry rather than the kind.

`docs/research/retro-tool-teardown.md` names this exact space as the largest unclaimed opening in the category: "Nobody ships *suggested* phases that carry real state — reveal policy, vote budget, discussion ordering — which a team can skip, reorder, or run ahead of without the tool breaking. That is the gap between 'structured' and 'rigid', and it is where the whole product sits."

## Considered Options

- **A soft machine — projection and default, never prohibition** (chosen). Keeps the evidenced benefit (structure and prompting) while removing the part no evidence supports (enforcement), and it is the one position in the category nobody occupies.
- **Rigid gating** (rejected). Retrium enforces one flow, force-follows every participant's screen, and offers Abstain-during-Vote as its sole opt-out anywhere; its own reviewers say "it is hard to host a creative retrospective with Retrium". Enforcement is where all of archetype 1's complaints come from, and none of it is evidence-backed.
- **Data-precondition locks on some stages** (rejected). Parabol keeps `GROUP` and `DISCUSS` locked until reflections and votes exist (`phasesWithExtraRequirements = [GROUP, DISCUSS]`). The failure it prevents — an empty screen — is not a failure; it is a screen with nothing on it yet, and it explains itself for the price of an empty state. The failure it causes is the loudest complaint in the teardown, "I lost the ability to move the cards".
- **No machine at all** (rejected). Spreo and FigJam delete the flow and make the human the entire runtime. This throws away the one thing the literature does support, and the teardown records canvas users asking for structure while structured-tool users ask for escape — both halves want the middle.
- **A code-constant sequence with per-retro enabled flags** (rejected). Parabol's `setMeetingSettings` exposes exactly three on/off switches and its four core phases cannot be turned off at all. Flags cannot express reordering or a per-format flow, and under permanent retention they cannot render an old retro with the sequence it was actually run with.

## Consequences

- **The UI carries the entire burden of legibility.** With nothing enforced, "what are we doing right now" is a design problem rather than a constraint problem. This is the real cost of the decision and it should not be discovered late.
- **A late card during Discuss is a feature, not an edge case.** It is frequently the best card in the retro. TeamRetro's "just leave it in Brainstorm" is the current state of the art for this and is a low bar.
- **Per-stage state hangs off the entry, not the kind**, because a kind may repeat. [Reveal policy](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/270) and the vote budget in [the retro data model](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/264) both inherit this, and it is what makes a genuinely per-stage reveal policy expressible.
- **The discussion walk snapshots its order on entry** — by votes descending where a `vote` stage ran, creation order otherwise. Votes are still accepted afterwards (nothing is forbidden); they simply do not reshuffle a walk in progress. Same write-time-snapshot shape as [ADR-0007](0007-analytics-read-from-a-write-time-snapshot.md). The walk tracks coverage rather than just position, because Lu et al. (65 studies, 3,189 groups) put information coverage against decision quality at **r = .56** and discussion focus at **r = .25**.
- **Nothing advances itself.** The **auto-reveal countdown** works because "every non-spectator has voted" is a completing predicate — one ballot per voter, and the roster only shrinks. "Everyone has finished writing" has no such predicate, and cutting generation short systematically removes the originality tail. An auto-advance here would only ever be a machine for interrupting people mid-sentence.
- **Timeboxes are advisory and never fire an advance.** Rogelberg et al. tested explicitly for a dose–response curve on meeting length and found none — "no curvilinear relationships (quadratic or cubic) were found" — so an enforcing timebox would be inventing a number and then obeying it. Reuse the pure math in `convex/timerState.ts`, not the `TimerNode` canvas row, which belongs to the React Flow canvas a retro board does not use.
- **Readiness stays ephemeral, and `collect` has none.** It lives in the presence payload, is shown named per person rather than as an aggregate, and clears when the pointer moves. During `collect` the only signal is whether someone has written a card, so no durable record of who declared themselves finished ever exists — that record would be a participation signal about a named person attached to a ceremony whose whole value depends on candour.
- **There is no finished state, and nothing downstream may ask for one.** Symmetry with there being no "not started": a retro rests wherever it was left. Carryover asks which actions are still open, not which retro closed; notifications ask whether the stage is `collect`. A finished flag would contradict the reopen-freely rule the first time anyone used it.
- **The backend has no stage guard to write, which means every retro mutation must be correct in every stage.** That is a real testing obligation, not a saving: the enforcement net is a test that each mutation behaves sanely when the board is in an unexpected stage, in the spirit of the cleanup tests named in [ADR-0005](0005-room-activity-has-one-model-layer-chokepoint.md).
- **A team can end up with two retros sitting in `collect` at once**, since the window opens at creation and nothing closes it automatically. Whether a team may have more than one live retro is the room-reuse and team-binding question owned by [the retro data model](https://github.com/spokvulcan/poker-planning/issues/264).
